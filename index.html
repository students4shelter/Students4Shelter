Why shelters need us

This is a housing emergency, not a personal failure.

Toronto shelters are running past capacity while funding stays flat. These are the numbers we bring into every classroom presentation we give.

300+deaths

people experiencing homelessness died in Toronto in a single year

That is roughly six people every week. Cold exposure, untreated illness and overdose are the leading causes, and almost all of them are preventable with shelter and care.

1 in 5is under 25

of Toronto’s homeless population is a young person

Thousands of youth cycle through couches, cars and shelter beds every year. Most left home to escape violence, family breakdown or being kicked out for who they are.

~11,000each night

people rely on Toronto’s shelter system every single night

The system was never built for this volume. Beds fill by early evening, and warming centres open only once the temperature has already turned dangerous.

200+turned away daily

calls for a shelter bed go unmatched on an average night

When Central Intake has nothing left to offer, the answer is a transit shelter, a stairwell or a ravine. Funding beds and outreach directly shrinks that number.

Figures are approximate and compiled from City of Toronto Shelter, Support & Housing Administration reporting, the Toronto Homeless Memorial and partner shelter data. We update them as new counts are published.

Our Mission

Students4Shelter is a youth-led community dedicated to humanizing homelessness and empowering the next generation of advocates. We strive to break systemic barriers and provide immediate aid to local shelters through collective youth action.

What we actually do

Money in shelter hands, and stories in people’s heads.

Funding alone does not end homelessness — indifference keeps it going. So we work on both ends at once: immediate aid for the shelters that need it tonight, and a long-term shift in how our city talks about the people inside them.

  • Fundraise with real events

    Bake sales, tournaments, thrift pop-ups and winter drives run entirely by students. Every dollar goes to a named Toronto shelter or drop-in partner.

  • Humanize homelessness

    We sit down with people living in shelters and encampments, record their stories with their consent, and publish them as short films and written portraits.

  • Build the next advocates

    Chapters in high schools and universities across the GTA learn how housing policy actually works, then bring it to their councils, classes and families.
